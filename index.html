Welcome

Exceptional care for the Hagerstown community.

Longmeadow Animal Hospital was established in its present location at 19764 Longmeadow Road, Hagerstown, MD in 1971. Since then, we have offered the highest quality veterinary medical care to the greater Hagerstown, Smithsburg, and Leitersburg area. We are dedicated to helping our companion animal friends live longer, healthier, and happier lives.

Here at Longmeadow Animal Hospital, you will find our staff is not only friendly and inviting, but also very qualified. We have worked hard to build a quality team — kind, understanding, and caring individuals who understand that a pet is more than just an animal, it is a friend.

Services

Everything your pet needs, one hospital.

Our goal is to improve the quality of your pet's life, and health. From annual wellness to surgery, diagnostics to end-of-life care — eighteen services, one team.

General Wellness

Prevention first

Wellness & Preventive Care

Annual wellness exams evaluate your pet's overall health, detect problems before they become serious, and keep them on track to live a long, healthy life.

Dentistry

Annual dental exams and cleanings are recommended to protect your pet from many health problems and help them maintain a healthy and clean mouth.

Vaccinations

Preventative care is an important aspect in maintaining your pet's health. Proper vaccination is vital in protecting them against harmful diseases.

Diet & Nutrition

Proper diet and nutrition can help your pet fight against disease, maintain a proper weight, and promote the overall well-being of your pet.

Parasite Control — Fleas, Ticks & Heartworms

Parasites such as fleas and ticks can be very damaging to your pet's health. Preventive measures should be taken year-round to inhibit potential outbreaks.

Microchipping

Microchip identification is the most reliable way to reunite lost pets with their owners.

Grooming

Grooming services for your dog — call the hospital for current scheduling.

Diagnostic Services

Answers, fast

Diagnostic Ultrasounds

An ultrasound is a highly useful tool when evaluating heart conditions, internal organs, cysts and tumors.

Radiography

We're equipped to perform routine radiography services to identify many types of illness or injury when pets are sick or suffer a trauma.

Laboratory Services

Diagnostic testing can identify problems your pet may be experiencing so that proper treatment can begin before a condition worsens.

Surgery Services

In-house, experienced hands

Spay & Neuter

Spaying or neutering your pet can help them live a longer, healthier life, minimize behavior problems, and help control the population of unwanted dogs and cats.

Soft Tissue Surgery

We perform soft tissue surgery for a number of medical reasons. This common surgery can be used for most anything non-joint or bone related.

Orthopedic Surgery

Orthopedic surgery can help pets who suffer from joint problems, torn ligaments, broken bones, and even help correct congenital problems.

Holistic & Therapy

Surgery-free, drug-free

Laser Therapy

A surgery-free, drug-free, noninvasive treatment to reduce pain and inflammation and speed the healing process — scientifically proven to help treat post-surgical pain and many acute and chronic conditions.

Laser Surgery

Laser surgery allows for the destruction of abnormal tissue and preserves normal tissue — gentle healing with little or no scarring.

Travel & End of Life

With you, every step

Domestic Health Certificates

Traveling domestically within the USA? As a federally accredited veterinarian practice, we can issue travel certificates for your healthy pet so they can travel with you.

Euthanasia

Euthanizing a pet is not an easy decision. We are here to discuss options and assist in every way we can during this difficult time.

Cremation

There are a number of wonderful ways to memorialize your beloved pet. Cremation can be a lovely way to honor and celebrate their lives.

Pet Safety

July 14, 2026

Dangers of Corn on the Cob

Summer cookouts, family barbecues, and fresh corn on the cob often go hand in hand. While corn itself isn't typically harmful for pets, the cob is a completely different story. Every year, veterinary hospitals treat dogs — and occasionally curious cats — for serious emergencies caused by swallowing corn cobs.

Plain, cooked corn kernels are generally safe for both dogs and cats in small amounts — corn is a common ingredient in many commercial pet foods, providing carbohydrates, fiber, vitamins and antioxidants. But corn on the cob should never be given to a pet: the cob is a choking hazard and can cause a life-threatening intestinal blockage that may require surgery.
